I am a tenure-track assistant professor in the Department of Computer Science at Illinois Institute of Technology (**Illinois Tech**). I earned my Ph.D. in Computer Science from the University of Pittsburgh, advised by [Dr. Youtao Zhang](https://people.cs.pitt.edu/~zhangyt/) and [Dr. Xulong Tang](https://xzt102.github.io/). I received my M.S. in Telecommunications from the University of Maryland, College Park, and my B.E. in Electrical Engineering and Automation from Beihang University, China.

My research develops **full-stack techniques across algorithms, runtimes, and hardware** to make AI systems more efficient and reliable. I pursue two connected goals: improving the systems that execute graph-based AI (**Systems for AI**) and using graph learning to guide efficient and reliable system design (**AI for AI Systems**). My current work spans three directions:

- **Efficient and reliable deep graph learning.** I develop algorithms and systems that reduce the training and inference costs of graph learning, from graph matching to temporal graph neural networks. Alongside these optimizations, I study how adversarial inputs affect graph models and their evolving states, with the goal of improving both efficiency and robustness.
- **Efficient graph-infused LLMs and agentic systems.** I study how graphs can organize external knowledge and experience without introducing excessive construction, retrieval, and execution costs. In GraphRAG, I investigate offline graph compression and adaptive online expansion. In agent memory, I study how to refine and filter past experience so agents can reuse efficient workflows.
- **Graph learning for efficient and reliable system design.** I investigate how learning can guide decisions about which computation to perform, reuse, or revisit. Building on my earlier work in learning-guided training control and graph-based reinforcement learning for quantum computing, I am extending this approach to selective error recovery in multi-agent systems and efficient image and video generation workflows.

I focus primarily on the first two directions, while developing the third as a complementary extension of my work on learning-guided system optimization.
